# A department chair's first session

This walkthrough is for a department chair who has been handed a schedule to
fill in for next year: the registrar made it, added your name, and now it is
your job to say what your department will teach. Follow it top to bottom once
and you will have done the whole loop — signed in, brought the schedule into
your week, added and adjusted courses, sent your suggestions, and seen what
other departments are asking for. Each step is enough to do the task; pages
with the finer details are linked at the bottom of the step and listed at the
end.

## 1. Sign in

When the app opens it asks you to **Sign in** or **Work offline**.

- **Sign in** keeps your schedules on the college server, so they are there
  next time and can be shared. If it offers **Sign in with SSO**, that is the
  college login you already use.
- **Work offline** keeps everything on this one computer — fine for trying
  things out, but nothing is shared.

Once you are in, the top right reads **Signed in as …**.

## 2. Show the schedule that was shared with you

Your schedule was created by someone else and your name was added to it. There
is no invitation link to click — it simply appears for you:

1. Click **Your schedules** at the top left.
2. Look under **Shared with you**, or under **Public** if it was opened to
   everyone. The **Filter by name or owner…** box and the **All years** list
   narrow things down.
3. Click the **eye** on its row.

The schedule is now showing in your week: its classes draw on the grid, and
the colored **pill** at the top names it. Nothing under **Shared with you**?
Ask the schedule's owner to add your name.

## 3. Read the week

The **Grid** is the home view: five columns, one per weekday (**M T W R F** —
**R is Thursday**), a time ruler down the left, and a colored block for each
class meeting.

- The **Fall**, **Winter**, and **Spring** buttons switch terms; next year's
  plan usually lives in one of them.
- Click a day name (for example **M Monday**) to see that whole day in order.
- Click a block to open it, then **View slot** to list everything meeting at
  that exact time.

## 4. Add your courses

You do not own this schedule, so you cannot change it directly — you
**suggest** changes and the owner approves them. That is how everyone else
works on it too.

1. On the schedule's colored **pill**, click the **speech bubble** to start
   suggesting. A bar appears with **＋ Add course**, **History**,
   **Propose changes**, and **Done**.

<Shot file="schedule-suggest-session.png" />

2. Click **＋ Add course** and start typing a course **code or name** — for
   example `CS 220` or `statistics`. The list narrows as you type. Searches
   lean toward the departments you belong to, but any course can be found.
3. Click the section you want (for example **CS 220 A**). The course editor
   opens with sensible defaults.

<Shot file="schedule-add-course.png" />

4. Adjust the teacher, meeting time, and seats if the defaults are not right
   (see the next step), then **Save changes**. The course appears in your week
   immediately — as a draft only you can see until you propose it.
5. Add the rest of your courses the same way.

Finer details: [Adding a course](/guide/schedule/add-course) and
[Editing vs suggesting](/guide/schedule/edit-vs-suggest).

## 5. Adjust an existing course

Click the **pencil** on any course block — or on its row in the table view —
to open the editor for that section. It holds everything:

- **Section** — the letter that identifies the class (A, B, …).
- **Seats** — the number of seats you are requesting (defaults to 24).
- **Title** — leave it blank to use the catalog title, or type one for a
  special-topics section.
- **Instructor** — start typing, then pick from the name suggestions.
- **Meeting time** — pick a standard **Time slot**, type a **Custom time**, or
  choose **No meeting time**.

<Shot file="schedule-course-editor.png" />

Click **Save changes** when you are done, or **Cancel** to leave the course
untouched. One quirk worth knowing: if a class meets **MWF** and you switch it
to **TR**, the old time no longer applies, so the app asks you to **Pick a
time slot for TR** before it will save — and the same the other way around.

Finer details: [Changing a course](/guide/schedule/edit-course), including
labs, co-teachers, and custom times.

## 6. Review your department in the table view

Switch to the **Table** tab to see every offering of the term as one compact
row: course, title, instructor, meeting, seats, core areas, and which schedule
it came from. It is the fastest way to check that your department adds up.

- **Departments** and **Instructors** chips hide everything that does not
  match; pick several chips to widen the list, and **Clear** to reset.
- In your suggest session the cells you may change are editable in place:
  click **Instructor** to pick from name suggestions, click **Meeting** to
  choose a standard time (or **Custom…**), and type over **Title** and
  **Seats**. Enter or clicking away commits; Esc cancels.
- The row's **pencil** opens the full course editor, and **＋ Add course** is
  right in the table's header.

<Shot file="schedule-table-edit.png" />

Finer details: [Working with the table view](/guide/schedule/table-view).

## 7. Send your suggestions

Nothing you have done so far has reached the schedule. Your edits sit in a
private draft until you send them:

1. Click **Propose changes** — the **●** beside it means there are changes not
   sent yet.
2. Add a short note if you like (for example "CS 220 moved to TR; CS 223
   added"), then send.
3. Click **Done** to leave the session. If anything is still unsent, the app
   asks before discarding it.

The proposals are now **pending**, waiting for the owner. Nothing is written
to the schedule until they approve.

Finer details: [Submitting changes](/guide/schedule/proposals).

## 8. See what other departments proposed

Suggestions run both ways. The toolbar's **Suggested changes** button opens
the shared record for the schedule: every live proposal, who made it, and the
history of what was approved, rejected, or withdrawn.

<Shot file="schedule-proposals.png" />

As a suggester (not the owner) you can read the whole trail here and withdraw
your own proposals; **Approve**, **Reject**, and **Export (markdown)** belong
to the owner. **History** on the session bar is different — that is just your
own changes from this sitting, with undo.

Finer details:
[Submitting changes and reviewing suggestions](/guide/schedule/proposals).
